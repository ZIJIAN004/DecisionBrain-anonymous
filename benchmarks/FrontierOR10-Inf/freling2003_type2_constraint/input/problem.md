# Problem Description

A logistics distribution network consists of a set of facilities and a set of customers. Each facility is a combined production plant and warehouse. The number of facilities, the number of customers, and the number of time periods in the planning horizon are all given. Each customer has a known total demand over the entire planning horizon. All customers share a common seasonal demand pattern described by a set of seasonal factors, one per period, that are nonnegative and sum to one. The demand of a given customer in a given period equals that customer's total demand multiplied by the seasonal factor for that period. Each facility has a known production capacity in each period. The cost of supplying a given customer from a given facility in a given period is specified. A unit inventory holding cost is given for each facility in each period.

Each customer must be assigned to exactly one facility for the entire planning horizon; a customer cannot split its demand across multiple facilities or switch facilities between periods. Each facility may serve any number of customers, as long as its production capacity and inventory levels permit. In each period, the total demand placed on a facility by its assigned customers, plus the inventory held at that facility at the end of that period, must not exceed the facility's production capacity in that period plus the inventory that was held at that facility at the end of the previous period. Every facility begins the planning horizon with zero inventory. Warehouses have unlimited physical storage space and unlimited throughput capacity, so any excess production can always be stored. Products may only be stored at facilities, never at customer locations.

The goal is to minimize total cost, which is the sum of two components: the total supply cost, obtained by summing over all periods, all facilities, and all customers the supply cost for each customer-facility pair in each period weighted by whether that customer is assigned to that facility, and the total inventory holding cost, obtained by summing over all periods and all facilities the unit holding cost times the amount of product stored at that facility at the end of that period.


## Additional binding constraints
In addition to all requirements above, customer 1 must be assigned to facility 1. Customer 1 must not be assigned to facility 1.
