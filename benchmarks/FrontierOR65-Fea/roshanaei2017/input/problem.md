# Problem Description

A coalition of hospitals collaboratively plans and schedules surgical operations over a multi-day planning horizon. The system involves a set of patients, a set of surgeons, a set of hospitals, a set of days forming the planning horizon, and a set of operating rooms (ORs) within each hospital. Each patient has a due date, a preparation time (pre-incision setup), an OR turnover/cleaning time incurred after their surgery, and a set of preferred surgeons who are qualified to operate on that patient. The surgical procedure time for a given patient depends on which surgeon performs the operation, so each patient-surgeon pair has a specific surgical procedure duration. The total time a patient occupies an OR equals the sum of the preparation time, the surgeon-specific surgical procedure time, and the cleaning time. Each patient is also eligible for only a subset of ORs at each hospital. Each surgeon has a designated set of days on which they operate and a specified number of available minutes on each of those days. Each OR at each hospital on each day has a known amount of regular (non-overtime) session time and a maximum allowable amount of overtime. Opening an OR incurs a fixed cost that depends on the specific OR, hospital, and day. Assigning a surgeon to operate at a particular hospital on a particular day incurs a fixed cost that depends on the surgeon, hospital, and day. Any overtime used in an OR is charged at a per-minute cost rate specific to that OR.

Patients are divided into two categories based on their due dates. Mandatory patients have due dates that fall within the planning horizon and must each be scheduled for surgery exactly once, on or before their due date. Optional patients have due dates that fall after the planning horizon and may each be scheduled at most once during the current horizon; scheduling an optional patient earns a reward whose magnitude is given as input and reflects the benefit of operating on that patient sooner rather than deferring them to a future planning cycle.

The planning authority must make the following decisions. First, it must assign each mandatory patient to exactly one combination of a qualified surgeon, a hospital, a day on or before the patient's due date, and an eligible OR at that hospital, and it may similarly assign each optional patient to at most one such combination on any day in the horizon. Second, it must decide which ORs to open at each hospital on each day and which surgeons to deploy to which hospitals on each day. Third, it must determine the amount of overtime used in each opened OR. Fourth, for each hospital-day, it must determine the sequence and timing of all surgical cases assigned to that hospital-day: specifically, the order in which patients are processed in each OR, the order in which each surgeon's cases are performed, and the precise finishing time of each patient's surgery.

On any given day, each surgeon may be assigned to at most one hospital. A patient can be assigned to a particular hospital-day-OR only if a qualified surgeon for that patient is present at that hospital on that day and only if the OR is open. The total time consumed by all surgeries assigned to a given OR on a given day, measured as the sum of the total OR-occupation times (preparation plus surgical procedure plus cleaning) of all patients in that OR, must not exceed the OR's regular session time plus whatever overtime is used, and the overtime used must not exceed the OR's maximum allowable overtime. Each surgeon's workload at a hospital on a given day is limited by the surgeon's available time on that day; this workload is measured as a weighted combination of surgical procedure times and total OR-occupation times, controlled by a schedule tightness parameter between zero and one, where a value of zero counts the full OR-occupation time toward the surgeon's capacity and a value of one counts only the surgical procedure time, with intermediate values interpolating between these extremes.

Within each hospital-day, the sequencing of operations must satisfy the following temporal rules. Each patient's surgery finishes no earlier than the sum of that patient's preparation time and the surgical procedure time corresponding to the assigned surgeon. If two patients are assigned to the same OR, they cannot overlap: one patient's surgery must finish entirely, including the subsequent cleaning time and the next patient's preparation time and surgical procedure, before the other's finishing time is reached, and vice versa through a pair of either-or precedence conditions. If two patients share the same surgeon, regardless of whether they are in the same or different ORs, the surgeon cannot operate on both simultaneously: one patient's surgery must be completed before the surgeon begins the procedure on the other, again enforced through a pair of either-or precedence conditions on finishing times and surgical procedure durations. The completion time of each OR is at least as large as the finishing time plus cleaning time of every patient assigned to that OR. The overtime of each OR is at least the amount by which the OR's completion time exceeds its regular session time, and overtime is bounded between zero and the maximum allowable overtime. Each surgeon has a starting time and an ending time at each hospital-day; the ending time is at least as large as the finishing time of every patient assigned to that surgeon, the starting time is at most the finishing time minus the surgical procedure time of every patient assigned to that surgeon, and the span from starting time to ending time must not exceed the surgeon's available time.

The goal is to minimize the total cost, defined as the sum of four components: the fixed costs of all opened ORs across all hospitals and days, the fixed costs of all surgeon-hospital-day assignments, the variable overtime costs across all ORs, minus the total rewards earned from scheduling optional patients in the current horizon.
