# Problem Description

A logistics network consists of m sources and n sinks. Each source has a known positive integer supply of goods that must be shipped out entirely, and each sink has a known positive integer demand that must be satisfied exactly. The total supply across all sources equals the total demand across all sinks. Every source may ship goods to every sink, forming a complete bipartite shipping network of m times n possible routes.

Two types of costs are associated with each route from a source to a sink. First, a per-unit transportation cost is incurred for every unit of goods shipped along that route. Second, a fixed cost is charged whenever any positive quantity of goods is shipped along that route, regardless of the quantity sent. The fixed cost represents charges such as toll fees, landing fees, setup costs, or road-building costs that are triggered by the mere use of a route. Each route also has a capacity equal to the smaller of the source's supply and the sink's demand, so that no route can carry more goods than the source has available or the sink requires.

The planner must determine how many units of goods to ship along each route. Every unit of supply at each source must be dispatched to one or more sinks, and every sink must receive exactly the quantity it demands. A route may carry any quantity from zero up to its capacity, but if any goods are shipped along a route, the corresponding fixed cost is incurred in full. The goal is to minimize the total cost, which is the sum over all used routes of the per-unit transportation cost times the quantity shipped plus the fixed cost for opening that route.
