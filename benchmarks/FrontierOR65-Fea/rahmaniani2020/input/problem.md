# Problem Description

A company must decide which facilities to open among a set of potential locations in order to serve a set of customers whose demands are uncertain. The uncertainty is represented by a finite set of scenarios, each occurring with a known probability. The input data specifies, for each potential facility, a fixed cost of opening, a service capacity, and geographic coordinates. For each customer, the data specifies geographic coordinates. For each facility-customer pair, a per-unit routing cost is given. For each scenario, a probability of occurrence is provided along with the demand of every customer under that scenario.

The company must choose which facilities to open and, for every scenario, how much flow to ship from each open facility to each customer. The goal is to minimize the sum of two cost components: the total fixed costs of opening the selected facilities, plus the expected routing cost, where the expected routing cost is the probability-weighted sum over all scenarios of the per-unit routing costs times the flows shipped in that scenario.

The following rules govern these decisions. In every scenario, each customer's total inbound flow from all facilities must be at least as large as that customer's demand under that scenario. In every scenario, each facility's total outbound flow to all customers must not exceed that facility's capacity if the facility is open, and must be zero if the facility is not open. Finally, the total capacity of all opened facilities must be at least as large as the maximum, taken over all scenarios, of the total demand of all customers in that scenario; this ensures that the opened facilities can collectively satisfy all customer demands regardless of which scenario is realized.
