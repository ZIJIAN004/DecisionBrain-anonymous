# Problem Description

A collection of items must be allocated among a set of knapsacks so as to maximize total profit. Each item has an individual profit (which may be negative) and a weight (which may be zero). Each distinct pair of items has a pairwise profit (which may also be negative), and the pairwise profit matrix is symmetric. Each knapsack has a positive integer capacity. The planner must decide, for each item, whether to place it in one of the knapsacks or to leave it unassigned. Each item may be placed in at most one knapsack. The total weight of items assigned to any single knapsack must not exceed that knapsack's capacity. The total profit to be maximized is the sum of two components: first, the individual profits of all items that are placed in some knapsack, and second, for every pair of distinct items that are placed together in the same knapsack, the pairwise profit associated with that pair.

The input data specifies the number of items, the number of knapsacks, a vector of individual profits (one integer per item, not necessarily nonnegative), a symmetric matrix of pairwise profits (one integer per unordered pair of distinct items, not necessarily nonnegative), a vector of item weights (one nonnegative value per item), and a vector of knapsack capacities (one positive integer per knapsack). The goal is to determine an assignment of items to knapsacks that maximizes the sum of the individual profits of all assigned items plus the sum of the pairwise profits of every co-assigned pair, while respecting the capacity of every knapsack and placing each item in at most one knapsack.
