# Problem Description

A distribution company operates a fleet of homogeneous vehicles from a single depot to serve a set of customers over a cyclic planning horizon. The planning horizon consists of a given number of days (num_days), where the last day is followed by the first day in a repeating cycle (planning_horizon_cyclic). A fixed number of identical vehicles (num_vehicles) is available each day, each with a common carrying capacity (vehicle_capacity) and a maximum allowable route duration (max_route_duration). The depot has a location specified by coordinates (x, y under depot), a time window during which vehicles must depart and return (time_window under depot), and a service time (service_time under depot). Each of the customers (num_customers in total) has a unique identifier (id), a location given by coordinates (x, y under customers), a daily demand quantity (demand_per_day), a time window within which service must begin (time_window under customers, indexed by [customer]), and a service time representing the duration of the stop (service_time under customers, indexed by [customer]). Each customer also has an original visit frequency indicating how many times per planning horizon the customer requires service (visit_frequency_original, indexed by [customer]).

Every customer offers a set of visiting patterns called schedules (schedules, indexed by [customer][schedule]). Each schedule specifies the days on which visits occur (days, indexed by [customer][schedule]) and the number of visits in the horizon (visit_frequency, indexed by [customer][schedule]). Each visit within a schedule is associated with a specific day (visit_day) and a number of consecutive demand-days that the visit covers (days_covered); the quantity delivered on that visit equals the customer's daily demand multiplied by the number of days covered (demand_per_visit). Distances and costs between all pairs of locations are computed as Euclidean distances based on the coordinates (distance_matrix_type, cost_type). Travel time between two locations equals the Euclidean distance plus the service time at the origin location.

The company must make two interrelated sets of decisions. First, for each customer, exactly one of the customer's offered schedules must be selected to determine on which days that customer will be visited and how much will be delivered at each visit. Second, for each day in the planning horizon, the company must design a set of vehicle routes, where each route is a sequence of customer visits starting and ending at the depot, determining which customers are served by which vehicle and in what order. The routes performed on each day must carry out exactly the visits prescribed by each customer's selected schedule: every visit (with its day and days-covered amount) required by a chosen schedule must be realized by exactly one route, and routes may not include visits that are not required by any chosen schedule.

On each day, the number of routes performed must not exceed the number of available vehicles. Each route must satisfy the following operational requirements: the total quantity delivered across all customers on the route must not exceed the vehicle capacity; the route duration must not exceed the maximum allowable route duration; service at each customer must begin within that customer's time window; the vehicle must depart from and return to the depot within the depot's time window; each visit included in the route must be compatible with at least one of that customer's offered schedules, meaning the combination of the visiting day and the number of demand-days covered must appear in at least one schedule offered by the customer; and each customer may appear at most once on any single route. A route's cost equals the total Euclidean distance traveled along its path, including the legs from the depot to the first customer, between consecutive customers, and from the last customer back to the depot.

The goal is to minimize the total routing cost, defined as the sum of the costs of all routes performed across all days of the planning horizon.
