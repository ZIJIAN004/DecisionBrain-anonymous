# Problem Description

An investor allocates a fixed pool of capital across a universe of securities. Each security has a known expected return per unit invested, and the pairwise risk of holding any two securities together is described by a symmetric covariance matrix whose eigenvalues are all at least zero. The covariance matrix may be supplied either directly or in factor-model form: a factor-loadings matrix (whose rows are factors and whose columns are securities) together with a per-security idiosyncratic-variance vector, in which case the full covariance equals the factor-loadings matrix multiplied by its transpose plus the diagonal matrix of idiosyncratic variances. Two trade-off parameters are also given: a return-weight that controls how strongly expected return is rewarded relative to risk, and a concentration parameter that controls how strongly the strategy is penalized for piling capital into too few securities.

The investor must choose what fraction of the capital each security receives. The fractions are non-negative (no short selling is allowed) and sum to one (the entire pool is invested). At most a given maximum number of securities may receive a positive fraction; this maximum is much smaller than the total number of securities. The problem may also include a list of linear rules on the chosen fractions, each supplied as a row of coefficients together with a lower bound and an upper bound on the corresponding weighted sum; such rules can encode sector-exposure limits, position-size caps, or a minimum required overall expected return. When per-security minimum and maximum thresholds are provided, each security either receives no investment at all or receives a fraction lying between its given minimum and its given maximum.

The goal is to minimize a total made up of three components. The first component is one half of the portfolio's variance, namely the symmetric weighted sum across every pair of securities of the corresponding covariance entry multiplied by the two chosen fractions. The second component is a concentration penalty equal to the sum of the squared chosen fractions divided by twice the concentration parameter. The third component, subtracted from the first two, is the return-weight multiplied by the portfolio's expected return, where the portfolio's expected return is the sum across securities of each security's expected return times its chosen fraction.
