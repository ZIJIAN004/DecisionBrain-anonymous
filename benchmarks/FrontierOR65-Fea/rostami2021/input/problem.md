# Problem Description

A transportation network consists of a given number of nodes that serve simultaneously as origins, destinations, and candidate hub locations. Every pair of nodes has a known distance between them. Each node has a fixed setup cost that is incurred if that node is selected to operate as a hub. Shipments between origin-destination pairs must be routed through the hub network: every shipment travels from its origin to a first hub (collection), possibly transfers to a second hub (inter-hub transfer), and then proceeds from that hub to its destination (distribution). The cost per unit of flow for the collection leg is the collection unit rate times the distance from origin to the first hub, the cost for the inter-hub leg is the transfer unit rate times the distance between the two hubs, and the cost for the distribution leg is the distribution unit rate times the distance from the second hub to the destination. Three unit cost rates are given as input: one for collection, one for inter-hub transfer, and one for distribution.

Demand is uncertain and is described by a finite set of scenarios, each with a known probability. In each scenario, the flow of items to be shipped from every origin to every destination is specified. The total outgoing flow from a node in a given scenario is the sum of its flows to all destinations under that scenario, and the total incoming flow to a node is the sum of flows from all origins to that node under that scenario.

The planner must make two types of decisions. First, before any scenario is realized, the planner selects which nodes to open as hubs; at least one hub must be opened. Second, after the demand scenario is revealed, the planner assigns every non-hub node to exactly one of the open hubs. A node that is itself a hub serves as its own hub and is not assigned elsewhere. A non-hub node may only be assigned to a node that has been opened as a hub. These assignment decisions are allowed to differ across scenarios, so the spoke-to-hub mapping can adapt to the realized demand.

The total cost has three components. The first component is the sum of fixed setup costs over all opened hubs, paid once regardless of the scenario. The second component, computed per scenario and weighted by the scenario probability, covers collection and distribution: for each non-hub node assigned to a hub, the cost is the distance from the node to its assigned hub multiplied by the sum of the collection rate times the node's total outgoing flow plus the distribution rate times the node's total incoming flow in that scenario. The third component, also computed per scenario and weighted by the scenario probability, covers inter-hub transfer: for each origin-destination pair with positive flow, the transfer cost is the transfer unit rate times the flow times the distance between the hub serving the origin and the hub serving the destination. When both the origin and its assigned hub are the same node (i.e., the origin is itself a hub), the first hub on the path is that origin node; similarly, when the destination is a hub, the second hub on the path is that destination node. For non-hub origins and destinations, the respective hubs are determined by the scenario-specific assignments.

The goal is to minimize the total expected cost, which is the sum of the fixed hub setup costs and the probability-weighted average across all scenarios of the collection, distribution, and inter-hub transfer costs. The problem also admits two variants. In the hub median variant, the fixed setup costs are removed and replaced by the requirement that exactly a prescribed number of hubs must be opened, where that number is a given integer. In the capacitated variant, each candidate hub node has a given capacity, and in every scenario the total outgoing flow of all nodes assigned to a hub (including the hub itself if it sends flow) must not exceed that hub's capacity.
