# Problem Description

A manufacturing company operates a three-level distribution network consisting of one production plant, a set of warehouses, and a set of retailers, over a finite planning horizon of discrete time periods. The plant is the sole source of a single product. Each warehouse is connected to the plant and serves a fixed subset of retailers; each retailer is linked to exactly one warehouse. There are no transfers between warehouses, nor between retailers. Goods flow exclusively from the plant to a warehouse and then from that warehouse to its assigned retailers.

Retailer demand is uncertain and is modeled through a finite set of scenarios, each with a known probability of occurrence. For every scenario, the demand of each retailer in each period is given. The company must make setup decisions before the demand uncertainty is resolved: for each facility (the plant, each warehouse, and each retailer) and each period, the company decides whether or not to activate that facility for production or ordering. Once the actual demands are revealed, the company determines, for each scenario, the quantities produced at the plant and ordered at each warehouse and retailer in each period, as well as the inventory held at each level at the end of each period. These quantity and inventory decisions are made separately for each scenario.

The planning approach tracks each distinct retailer-period demand separately through the network. For every retailer, every demand period, and every scenario, the company must determine, for each earlier-or-equal production/order period, how much is produced at the plant, how much is ordered at the warehouse, and how much is ordered at the retailer in that production/order period to serve that particular retailer-period demand under that scenario. Likewise, inventory at each level is tracked per retailer-period demand: for each retailer, demand period, scenario, and each earlier-or-equal period, the stock held at the plant, the warehouse, and the retailer at the end of that period for that retailer-period demand is recorded.

Inventory balances must hold at every level for each retailer-period demand. At the plant level, the quantity shipped from the plant to the warehouse in a given period for a given retailer-period demand, plus the plant inventory of that retailer-period demand at the end of that period, equals the plant inventory from the previous period plus the quantity produced in that period for that retailer-period demand. At the warehouse level, the quantity shipped from the warehouse to the retailer in a given period for a given retailer-period demand, plus the warehouse inventory of that retailer-period demand at the end of that period, equals the warehouse inventory from the previous period plus the quantity received from the plant in that period for that retailer-period demand. At the retailer level, in the period when the demand is actually due, the demand itself is consumed; in all earlier periods, retailer inventory of that retailer-period demand carries forward. The retailer inventory from the previous period plus the quantity received from the warehouse equals either the scenario demand (when the current period matches the demand period) or the retailer inventory at the end of the current period (when the current period is strictly before the demand period).

Production and ordering quantities are linked to setup decisions. The quantity produced at the plant in any period for any retailer-period demand under any scenario cannot exceed the scenario demand for that retailer-period demand multiplied by whether the plant is set up in that period. Similarly, the quantity ordered at a warehouse in a period for a retailer-period demand cannot exceed the scenario demand for that retailer-period demand multiplied by whether that warehouse is set up in that period, and the quantity ordered at a retailer in a period for a retailer-period demand cannot exceed the scenario demand multiplied by whether that retailer is set up in that period.

All inventories start at zero across all facilities, retailer-period demands, and scenarios. Additionally, in the first period, all facilities must be set up: the plant, every warehouse, and every retailer must each have their setup activated in the first period.

All indices serialized in the solution keys are zero-based. Periods are `0,...,num_periods-1`, scenarios are `0,...,num_scenarios-1`, warehouses are `0,...,num_warehouses-1`, and retailers are `0,...,num_retailers-1`. In setup-variable keys, facility `0` is the plant, facilities `1,...,num_warehouses` are the warehouses in input order, and facilities `num_warehouses+1,...,num_warehouses+num_retailers` are the retailers in input order. Consequently, "the first period" means period `0`: when `initial_setups_imposed` is true, the solution must contain `y_{facility}_0 = 1` for every facility. Use these same zero-based indices in all serialized keys: `y_{facility}_{period}` for setup variables, `{period}_{scenario}` for plant quantities, `{warehouse}_{period}_{scenario}` for warehouse quantities, and `{retailer}_{period}_{scenario}` for retailer quantities.

The input data specifies the number of periods, the number of warehouses, the number of retailers, and the assignment of each retailer to its warehouse. It provides the number of demand scenarios and the probability of each scenario. For each scenario, the demand of each retailer in each period is given. Setup costs are provided for the plant in each period, for each warehouse in each period, and for each retailer in each period. Holding costs per unit per period are given for the plant, for each warehouse, and for each retailer; holding costs are nondecreasing along the supply chain from plant to warehouse to retailer.

The goal is to minimize the total expected cost, which is the sum of two components. The first component is the total setup cost: the sum over all facilities and all periods of the setup cost at that facility in that period multiplied by whether that facility is set up. The second component is the expected inventory holding cost: the probability-weighted sum over all scenarios of the total holding cost incurred in that scenario, where the holding cost in a scenario is the sum over all demand periods, all retailers, and all earlier-or-equal stock-holding periods of the per-unit holding cost at the plant in that stock-holding period times the plant stock for that retailer-period demand, plus the per-unit holding cost at the warehouse in that stock-holding period times the warehouse stock for that retailer-period demand, plus the per-unit holding cost at the retailer in that stock-holding period times the retailer stock for that retailer-period demand. No production capacity limits are imposed at any facility.
