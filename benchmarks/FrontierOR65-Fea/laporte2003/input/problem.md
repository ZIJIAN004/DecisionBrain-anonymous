# Problem Description

A purchaser based at a depot must acquire a set of products by visiting a subset of markets and then return to the depot, traveling along a route that forms a simple cycle (a round trip visiting each selected market exactly once). There are n markets (with n at least four) and m products (with m at least one). The depot and the markets together form the vertices of a complete undirected graph, and every pair of vertices is connected by an edge with a known symmetric travel cost. Each product has a required demand specifying how many units must be purchased in total. Each product is available at only a specific subset of the markets, and for each product-market pair where the product is available, the data specifies both a per-unit purchase price and a supply limit giving the maximum number of units of that product available at that market. Each supply limit is strictly positive and does not exceed the demand for that product, and the total supply of each product across all markets where it is available is at least as large as the demand for that product.

The purchaser must decide which markets to visit, how much of each product to buy at each visited market, and the order in which to travel through the selected markets, forming a single round-trip cycle that starts and ends at the depot. The total amount of each product purchased across all visited markets must exactly equal the demand for that product. A product may only be purchased at a market that the purchaser actually visits, and the amount purchased there cannot exceed the supply available at that market.

Certain markets are mandatory: a market must be visited if there exists some product for which the remaining supply at all other markets that carry that product would be insufficient to meet demand without that market. The depot is always included in the route. The route must form a connected simple cycle, meaning that for every subset of markets in the route, there must be sufficient edges connecting that subset to the rest of the route to maintain connectivity; specifically, for any subset of selected markets and any selected market within that subset, at least two edges of the route must cross between that subset and the remaining vertices. Each visited vertex has exactly two route edges incident to it, and each unvisited market has no route edges incident to it.

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which is the sum of all travel costs along the edges used in the route plus the total purchase cost of all products bought across all markets visited.
