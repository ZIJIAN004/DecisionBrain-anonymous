# Problem Description

A steel mill holds a surplus inventory of slabs and an order book of customer orders. The planner must decide which orders to fulfill from which surplus slabs, with the twin goals of maximizing the total weight of orders satisfied from inventory and minimizing the unused (wasted) weight on every slab that is put into service. There are a given number of slabs and a given number of orders. Each order has a known weight and a single color attribute representing the finishing-line route code that governs the sequence of process operations the order requires. Each slab has a known weight that acts as its capacity. For every order, a specified subset of slabs is eligible to supply it, determined by quality grade and physical dimension compatibility; an order may only be assigned to a slab in its eligible subset. Each slab likewise has a known set of orders eligible for assignment to it, and a known set of distinct colors among those eligible orders.

The planner must choose, for each order, whether to assign it to one of its eligible slabs or to leave it unassigned, and must also determine which slabs are placed into service. An order may be assigned to at most one slab. Orders may only be assigned to a slab that is placed into service, and the total weight of all orders assigned to any in-service slab must not exceed that slab's weight. Because cutting slabs between order types is expensive and the cutting machine is a bottleneck, at most two distinct colors may appear among the orders assigned to any single slab. An order of a given color can be assigned to a slab only if that color is one of the (at most two) colors selected for that slab.

The goal is to maximize the sum, over all assigned orders, of twice the order weight, minus the sum, over all slabs placed into service, of the slab weight. Equivalently, this objective simultaneously rewards fulfilling heavy orders and penalizes the full weight of every slab that is opened, so that a slab is worth opening only when the orders packed onto it recover enough weight to offset the slab's cost. A slab that has no orders assigned to it is not considered in service and incurs no penalty.

The input data consists of the number of slabs, the number of orders, the number of distinct colors, the weight of each slab, the weight of each order, the color of each order, and for each order the list of slabs that are eligible to supply it. The maximum number of distinct colors permitted on any single slab is also provided as an input (set to two in the instances studied).
