# Problem Description

A delivery company operates a homogeneous fleet of vehicles from a single depot to serve a set of customers. The depot and each customer are located at known positions in a two-dimensional plane, specified by their x- and y-coordinates. Each customer has a known demand quantity, a time window defined by an earliest service start time (ready time) and a latest arrival time (due date), and a fixed service duration that the vehicle must spend at the customer's location once service begins. The depot itself has a time window representing the scheduling horizon within which all vehicle operations must occur, zero demand, and zero service time. All vehicles are identical and share the same carrying capacity. There is no limit on the number of vehicles that may be used.

The company must select a collection of delivery routes, where each route is performed by one vehicle, begins at the depot, visits a sequence of one or more customers, and returns to the depot. Every customer must be visited by at least one route. A route is considered operationally valid only if the total demand of all customers assigned to that route does not exceed the vehicle's carrying capacity, and the vehicle respects every customer's time window along the route. Specifically, a vehicle traveling a route must arrive at each customer no later than that customer's due date. If the vehicle arrives at a customer before the customer's ready time, the vehicle must wait until the ready time before beginning service. Travel times between any two locations equal their Euclidean distances. After completing service at one customer (which takes the full service duration), the vehicle departs immediately to the next customer on the route or returns to the depot.

The goal is to determine which routes to operate so that every customer is served while minimizing the total travel distance across all selected routes, computed as the sum of the Euclidean distances traversed along each route.
