# Problem Description

A power system operator must plan the expansion of generation, transmission, and energy storage infrastructure over a multi-stage planning horizon while simultaneously scheduling the hourly operation of all generation and storage units. The system consists of a set of buses connected by a transmission network. At each bus there may be existing generators, potential (candidate) generators, existing storage devices, potential storage devices, and electricity demand. Buses are grouped into reserve margin regions. The planning horizon is represented as a scenario tree, where each node corresponds to a stage and captures uncertainty in investment costs and long-run demand growth; each node has a known probability of being reached from the root. Within each scenario-tree node, system operations are evaluated over a set of representative days (capturing seasonal and weekday/weekend variation), and each representative day consists of a fixed number of hours. At the operational level, hourly renewable energy output and electricity demand are uncertain and modeled through multiple sampled realizations per representative day.

The input data specifies, for each bus, the set of existing and candidate generators along with their maximum and minimum power output levels (in MW), start-up costs, quadratic generation cost function coefficients (a constant term, a linear coefficient, and a quadratic coefficient applied to hourly power output), ramp-up and ramp-down rates (in MW per hour), and a minimum up-and-down time (in hours) that applies equally to on and off periods. Each candidate generator, transmission line, and storage device has a rated capacity (in MW) and a construction cost per unit capacity at each scenario-tree node. Each generator has a derating factor expressing the fraction of its nameplate capacity that counts toward resource adequacy. The data includes, for each existing and candidate transmission line, the flow limit (in MW) and the percentage of energy lost when power is transmitted along that line. For each existing and candidate storage device, a withdrawal efficiency (the fraction of injected energy that can later be withdrawn) and a rated energy capacity are given. Each bus with load has a peak demand value, and hourly demand at each bus in each representative day is specified as a fraction of peak demand via a load profile. Solar generators have hourly capacity profiles, and wind generators have Weibull distribution parameters from which hourly output realizations are sampled. A reserve margin requirement (as a percentage above peak demand) is specified for each reserve margin region, and a spinning reserve requirement (as a percentage of hourly demand) is specified for each bus.

The operator must decide, at each scenario-tree node, which candidate generators, transmission lines, and storage devices to build. These are once-and-for-all decisions: each candidate infrastructure may be built at most once over the entire planning horizon. The available capacity of each candidate infrastructure at a given scenario-tree node equals its available capacity at the predecessor node plus its rated capacity if it is built at the current node, and this available capacity can never exceed the rated capacity. At the root node, all candidate available capacities start at zero. For each representative day within each scenario-tree node, and for each sampled realization of hourly uncertainties, the operator must also decide the hourly commitment status (on or off) and start-up status of every generator, the hourly power output and spinning reserve contribution of every generator, the hourly energy withdrawal from and injection into every storage device, the hourly stored energy level in every storage device, and the hourly power flow on every transmission line.

A candidate generator may only be committed in a given hour if it has been built by that scenario-tree node; specifically, its commitment status in any hour cannot exceed the ratio of its available capacity to its rated capacity (which is either zero or one). Once a generator is turned on, it must remain on for at least its minimum up-and-down time; for every hour in which a generator transitions from off to on, its commitment status must remain at one for at least the specified number of consecutive hours (or through the end of the day if fewer hours remain). Symmetrically, once a generator is turned off, it must remain off for at least the same number of hours; for every hour in which a generator transitions from on to off, its commitment status must remain at zero for at least the minimum up-and-down time in consecutive hours (or through the end of the day if fewer hours remain). A generator is recognized as starting up in an hour if its commitment status in that hour exceeds its commitment status in the previous hour; the start-up indicator must be at least as large as this difference. The sum of a generator's power output and spinning reserve contribution in any hour is bounded above by the maximum power output times the commitment status for existing generators, or by the available capacity for candidate generators. When a generator is committed, its power output must be at least its minimum power output times the commitment status. The total spinning reserve provided by all generators at each bus must meet or exceed the spinning reserve requirement at that bus in every hour. The change in a generator's power output between consecutive hours is limited by its ramp-up rate (for increases) and ramp-down rate (for decreases).

Each storage device begins each representative day with zero stored energy. In each subsequent hour, the stored energy equals the previous hour's stored energy plus the withdrawal efficiency times the energy injected in the previous hour minus the energy withdrawn in the previous hour. Energy withdrawn from a storage device in any hour cannot exceed its current stored energy. For candidate storage devices, the stored energy in any hour cannot exceed the available capacity established by the expansion decisions at the current scenario-tree node.

At every bus and every hour, energy balance must hold: the demand at the bus plus the total energy sent out on all lines departing that bus minus the total energy received on all lines arriving at that bus (after subtracting transmission losses) must equal the total power generated at that bus plus the net withdrawal (withdrawal minus injection) from all storage devices at that bus. The power flow on each transmission line in every hour is limited by the line's capacity, which for existing lines is the given flow limit and for candidate lines is the available capacity from the expansion decisions.

In each reserve margin region, the sum of the derated capacities of all existing generators plus the derated available capacities of all candidate generators across all buses in the region must be at least one plus the reserve margin requirement times the peak demand in that region at the current scenario-tree node.

The goal is to minimize the probability-weighted sum, over all scenario-tree nodes, of the total investment cost at that node plus the expected total operating cost across all representative days and sampled realizations at that node. The investment cost at a node is the sum of construction costs for all generators, transmission lines, and storage devices built at that node. The operating cost for a representative day under a given realization is the sum over all hours of the start-up costs for generators that start up in that hour plus the generation cost for each generator, where the generation cost is a function of the power output and the spinning reserve contribution, equal to a constant term plus a linear coefficient times the power output plus a quadratic coefficient times the power output squared.
