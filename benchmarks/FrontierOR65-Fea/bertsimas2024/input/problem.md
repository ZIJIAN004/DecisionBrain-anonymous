# Problem Description

A logistics planner manages a capacitated directed network consisting of a set of nodes and a set of directed edges (arcs) connecting pairs of nodes. Multiple distinct commodities must be shipped through this network. Demand for these commodities is uncertain and is represented by a collection of historical demand scenarios, each considered equally likely. For every commodity and every scenario, each node in the network has a known net supply or demand value: a positive value indicates that the node supplies that commodity, and a negative value indicates that the node consumes it.

The planner must make two kinds of decisions. First, the planner selects which edges in the network to activate (construct). Second, for each demand scenario, the planner determines the quantity of each commodity to route along each activated edge. The input data includes, for every edge, a fixed construction cost incurred if that edge is activated, a per-unit transportation cost for shipping each commodity along that edge, and a capacity limiting the total flow of all commodities combined that the edge can carry. A regularization parameter gamma (strictly positive) is also given, along with an upper limit on the total number of edges that may be activated.

Flow must be conserved at every node: for each commodity in each scenario, the net flow into and out of every node must equal the node's net supply or demand value for that commodity in that scenario. The total flow of all commodities on any edge in any scenario must not exceed that edge's capacity. No commodity may be routed along an edge that has not been activated; an edge carries zero flow of every commodity in every scenario unless it has been selected for construction. The total number of activated edges across the entire network must not exceed the given upper limit.

The goal is to minimize total cost, which consists of three components summed together. The first component is the total construction cost, equal to the sum of fixed construction costs over all activated edges. The second component is the expected transportation cost, computed as the average across all scenarios of the sum over all edges of the per-unit transportation costs multiplied by the corresponding commodity flows. The third component is an expected quadratic congestion penalty, computed as the average across all scenarios of the sum over all edges of one over twice gamma multiplied by the square of the total commodity flow on that edge. This quadratic term penalizes heavy utilization of individual edges and acts as a soft reinforcement of the capacity limits.
