# Problem Description

A single supplier distributes a single commodity to a set of customers over a finite planning horizon consisting of a given number of discrete periods, using a homogeneous fleet of vehicles each with a known carrying capacity. The supplier produces a known quantity of the commodity in each period, and each customer consumes a known quantity (its demand) in each period. Both the supplier and every customer have a maximum inventory capacity and an initial inventory level that does not exceed that capacity. Each location — the supplier and every customer — has a per-unit holding cost charged on its end-of-period inventory. A symmetric travel cost is specified for every pair of locations, including between the supplier and each customer, and between every pair of customers.

In each period the supplier first produces its output, then dispatches vehicles to make deliveries. Customers receive deliveries at the beginning of the period and may use the delivered goods to satisfy that period's demand. The supplier must decide, for each period, which customers to visit, what quantity to deliver to each visited customer, and how to combine the visits into vehicle routes that each start and end at the supplier. Any quantity may be delivered to a customer as long as the customer's inventory capacity is never exceeded; there is no requirement to fill the customer's storage completely upon a visit. No customer may experience a stockout in any period: the combination of its on-hand inventory and any delivery received must cover its demand in every period.

Each route spans a single period, departs from and returns to the supplier, and the total quantity loaded onto a vehicle for the route must not exceed the vehicle capacity. Each customer may be visited by at most one vehicle in any given period. The total number of routes operated in any period must not exceed the number of available vehicles. The supplier's inventory at the end of every period must remain between zero and its maximum capacity, inclusive.

The supplier's inventory must balance across periods: the inventory at the end of any period equals the inventory at the end of the previous period plus that period's production minus the total quantity loaded onto all vehicles dispatched in that period. Each customer's inventory must likewise balance across periods: the inventory at the end of any period equals the inventory at the end of the previous period plus the quantity delivered to the customer in that period minus the customer's demand in that period.

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which is the sum of two components: the total travel cost incurred by all vehicle routes across all periods, plus the total inventory holding cost at all locations (supplier and customers) across all periods, where each location's holding cost in a period equals its per-unit holding cost multiplied by its end-of-period inventory level.
