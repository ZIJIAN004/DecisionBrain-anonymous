# Problem Description

A team of defending robots and a team of attacking robots operate on a two-dimensional playing field with a circular Defense Zone of radius R_dz centered at the origin. There are N_D defenders and N_A attackers. Each attacker moves in a straight line toward the Defense Zone at a constant velocity. Each defender is governed by linear discrete-time dynamics and is steered by choosing control inputs at each time step. The objective is to find a control-input sequence for every defender that minimizes the number of time steps during which attackers occupy the Defense Zone, with an optional small penalty on the defenders' cumulative control effort.

Time is discretized. Defenders take N_u control steps at a uniform interval T_u, and attackers are tracked at N_a sample steps at a uniform interval T_a. Each defender has a two-dimensional position and two-dimensional velocity. Between consecutive control steps the defender's state evolves linearly according to the following first-order dynamics: the velocity decays toward the applied control input exponentially with time constant one, and the position is the integral of that velocity. Concretely, the next-step velocity in each coordinate equals the current velocity times exp(−T_u) plus the control input times (1 − exp(−T_u)), and the next-step position in each coordinate equals the current position plus the current velocity times (1 − exp(−T_u)) plus the control input times (T_u − 1 + exp(−T_u)). Each defender's initial position and velocity are given.

Each defender's control input at every step is a two-component vector whose magnitude is at most one (lies in the unit disk). Each attacker moves at constant velocity while active: its position at the next sample step equals its current position plus its velocity times T_a. An attacker becomes inactive (and stops moving thereafter) as soon as it either enters the Defense Zone or is intercepted by at least one defender.

An attacker is considered inside the Defense Zone at a given step if its position lies within the circle of radius R_dz centered at the origin. An attacker is considered intercepted by a defender at a given step if, at the attacker's sample time, the attacker's position lies within a circular intercept region of radius R_I centered at the defender's interpolated position at that time (linearly interpolated from the surrounding control-step states and inputs).

Defenders must stay outside the Defense Zone at all times. A set of N_o obstacle-avoidance check times within the drill horizon is given; at each such time, every defender's interpolated position must lie outside the Defense Zone.

The objective to minimize is the total number of attacker-time-step pairs during which the attacker occupies the Defense Zone (summed over all attackers and all sample steps from 1 through N_a), plus a non-negative weight epsilon times the total control effort (summed over defenders, control steps, and control coordinates, with effort measured as the sum of absolute values of control components). The weight epsilon is zero when only zone penetration is penalized, or a small positive number (such as 0.1) when fuel conservation is an additional secondary goal.

The input data for an instance specifies: the number of defenders N_D and attackers N_A; the Defense Zone radius R_dz; the intercept region radius R_I; the numbers of discretization steps N_u, N_a, N_o; the uniform time steps T_u, T_a; the cost weight epsilon; for each defender its initial position and velocity in two dimensions; and for each attacker its initial position and constant velocity vector in two dimensions.
